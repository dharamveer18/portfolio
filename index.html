Work

Where my three years have gone.

Two of these were built for my company’s clients and stay unnamed under NDA, so there are no logos or screens. The other two are open — the live site and the Figma file are both linked, so click around.

ERP · under NDA

Inventory and purchase ERP for a clothing brand

Every movement in the business gets an entry: raw material arriving, finished stock going out, and the vendor, buyer or party on the other side of it. The system holds the full history behind each item, so the team can answer what came in, what left, who it went to and what is actually on the floor right now — without anyone opening a spreadsheet. Built in Python and Django.

  • Python
  • Django
  • Inventory & stock
  • Reporting
Live

Village in India — census figures for every village in the country

A public reference site covering all 28 states down to the district, tehsil and village: population, sex ratio, literacy, area, pincode and tribal data, plus plain-Hindi guides to the government services rural households actually use. I collected the Census 2011 data by scraping, cleaned it into a structured database and built the whole site around it — 590,364 villages across 5,676 tehsils. Python on the back end, Tailwind CSS on the front.

  • Python
  • Tailwind CSS
  • Web scraping
  • Census data
villageinindia.com
Web + app · under NDA

Site, admin panel and table-ordering app for a hotel and restaurant chain

The public site shows the property, the rooms and the banquet halls, and takes bookings and enquiries. Behind it, an admin panel where the team sees every enquiry as it lands and tracks sales. Alongside it, an app for the restaurant: a guest scans the code on their table, gets the full menu on their phone and orders from their seat. Python on the back end, React Native for the app.

  • Python
  • React Native
  • Bookings & enquiries
  • Admin dashboard
Figma file

Study Hive — a student organiser app, researched and designed in Figma

A cross-platform organiser for students aged 18–25 trying to hold coursework, deadlines and everything else in one place. The design started with an online survey — 83% of respondents wanted deadline reminders, 71% wanted calendar and schedule management — then personas, an empathy map, user flow and information architecture, a style guide built on a deep red primary and Work Sans, and hi-fi screens for tasks, assignments, courses and progress analysis. Seven students tested the prototype over Discord; their feedback cut a redundant add/create screen and split tasks into proper status categories.

Process

How a project usually goes.

Four stages, no week where you wonder what’s happening.

  1. 01

    We talk

    A call or a long email. What you need, what it’s worth to you, and whether I’m the right person. Sometimes the answer is no.

  2. 02

    I scope it

    A written plan: what’s included, what it costs, roughly how long. No surprise invoices later.

  3. 03

    I build, you watch

    Something clickable early, and updates as it goes, so nothing lands as a shock at the end.

  4. 04

    Launch, then improve

    Deploy, measure, fix what the numbers point at. I stay reachable afterwards.
